Functional screening and actuarial pricing for DNA synthesis biosecurity

A working prototype that catches AI-designed toxins by function rather than sequence, and prices the risk through a self-financing actuarial mechanism that funds global biosecurity capacity.

Executive summary

DNA synthesis screening is the primary chokepoint preventing the synthesis of hazardous biological material. Every major provider runs customer orders through sequence-similarity tools. These tools are effective against known sequences. They fail systematically against AI-designed variants that rewrite the sequence while preserving function.

A 2025 Microsoft-led red-teaming study demonstrated that up to 100% of AI-generated ricin variants evaded major commercial screening pipelines. The mechanism is straightforward: ricin's toxicity is localized to a small active site. Everything else is scaffolding. A generative model can rewrite the scaffolding while preserving the active site.

BioScreen is a two-layer response. Layer 1 embeds query proteins using ESM-C 600M and computes a contrastive hazard score — the difference between best-match similarity to a reference set of 16 controlled toxins and best-match similarity to 25 safe reference proteins. Layer 2 converts the screening signal plus order context into a risk-priced premium, using catastrophe-insurance methods to fund biosecurity capacity through a 0.12% loading on synthesis orders.

Headline result

On a mutation-based variant-detection benchmark, BioScreen catches 100% of 1–3-mutation variants and 83% of 10-mutation variants — the divergence range where standard sequence screening begins to miss. Specificity on 60 fresh human proteins is 96.7% at the calibrated operating point. Everything is computed live from ESM-C embeddings and UniProt sequences. Nothing is hardcoded.

What BioScreen is — and is not

  • Is: a complementary second layer to existing sequence-based screening.
  • Is: an economic mechanism that makes transparency profitable.
  • Is: fully open-source, reproducible from public data, deployable on commodity hardware.
  • Is not: a replacement for BLAST-based screening. Both layers should run in parallel.
  • Is not: a regulatory-grade validated tool. It is a proof-of-concept for accelerator-phase work.
  • Is not: a full solution to AI evasion. It raises the cost of evasion; it does not eliminate it.

The problem

Global DNA synthesis volume is on the order of 5 million orders per year, representing a market of roughly $5B. Every order passes through screening at the provider level. The screening infrastructure is the last technical chokepoint before synthetic DNA reaches a laboratory.

The AI evasion gap

Protein design tools — RFdiffusion, ProteinMPNN, ESM-IF, and others — can now generate protein sequences that fold to target structures or perform target functions without matching any natural sequence. Applying these to known toxins produces variants that are functionally identical but sequentially novel. The 2025 Microsoft red-teaming study tested this directly against commercial screening pipelines, with up to 100% evasion.

The structural gap

Sequence screening asks "does this look like a known toxin?". AI-designed variants change the look. The question itself is the wrong one.

Related vulnerabilities

  • Order fragmentation. A hazardous gene split across multiple innocuous orders.
  • Benchtop synthesis. Desktop synthesizers bypass commercial screening entirely.
  • Database lag. Newly characterized toxins take months to appear in screening sets.
  • No economic layer. No mechanism prices biosecurity risk or rewards transparency.

Method

BioScreen has two layers. Layer 1 answers the technical question: does this protein function like a known toxin? Layer 2 answers the economic question: how should biosecurity risk be priced, and who pays?

Layer 1 — Functional screening

Query proteins are embedded with ESM-C 600M (EvolutionaryScale, MIT license). The full per-residue embedding matrix (L, 1152) is preserved — no mean pooling. Each residue keeps its own 1152-dimensional vector.

For each reference protein in the toxin and safe sets, we compute a local alignment score: for every query residue, find its best match in the reference, take the mean of the top-50 per-residue matches. The contrastive hazard score is the difference between the best toxin match and the best safe match: 

hazard_delta  =  max_toxin align(Q, t)  −  max_safe align(Q, s)

flagged  =  (hazard_delta >= 0.02)  AND  (best_toxin_sim >= 0.90)

Both gates must open for a sequence to flag. The two-gate design eliminates false positives from proteins that weakly resemble a toxin.

Layer 2 — Actuarial pricing

The screening output feeds a catastrophe-insurance pricing engine. Risk is computed as a screening signal modulated by order context: 

risk  =  screening_base  ×  context_modulator
premium  =  order_cost  ×  loading(risk)

Tier thresholds are calibrated so legitimate orders fall in LOW, suspicious orders fall in MEDIUM, and flagged orders fall in HIGH or CRITICAL. Verified customers pay 65% less than unverified — the incentive-compatibility property.

Operating point

Calibrated threshold — 0.02

Chosen to balance LOO sensitivity (69%) against specificity (96.7%). The previous default of 0.05 was rejected because it gave LOO sensitivity of only 31%. Variant-detection sensitivity is largely insensitive to this choice (83–85% across the range). The threshold is a policy parameter, configurable per deploying provider.

Limitations

LimitationImpactMitigation / roadmap
Reference set is small (16 toxins, 25 safes) LOO sensitivity is 69% — a toxin with no sibling in the database may be missed Expand to 100+ toxins covering all Australia Group categories (accelerator phase)
Narrow margin at high thresholds At 0.05, LOO sensitivity drops to 31% Calibrate operating point against the provider's risk tolerance
Synthetic order simulation Pricing extrapolation uses synthetic distributions Calibrate against real order data from an industry partner
No wet-lab confirmation All results computational In vitro validation of a subset of flagged variants ($50K scope)
Bounded adversarial robustness An attacker with the tool could iterate designs Complementary deployment; per-provider thresholds; managed signature set; human review

Roadmap

Accelerator phase — 6 months

  • Structural similarity layer (ESMFold + FoldSeek). Target margin > 0.15.
  • Expanded reference database. 16 → 100+ controlled toxins.
  • Open benchmark. Reproducible benchmark of AI-designed variants.
  • Industry pilot. One synthesis provider or CRO with real order data.
  • Pricing calibration against real distributions.
  • Regulatory engagement with WHO, BWC ISU, or national authorities.

Beyond the accelerator — 12–24 months

  • Production deployment with a synthesis provider.
  • Regulatory recognition as a valid screening signal.
  • First LMIC lab subsidies disbursed through the mechanism.
  • Community-governed reference set.
  • Extension to benchtop synthesizers.
