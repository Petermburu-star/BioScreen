Section 1

Executive summary

DNA synthesis screening is the primary chokepoint preventing the synthesis of hazardous biological material. Every major provider runs customer orders through sequence-similarity tools — BLAST, DIAMOND, and curated hazard databases. These tools are effective against known sequences. They fail systematically against AI-designed variants that rewrite the sequence while preserving function.

A 2025 Microsoft-led red-teaming study demonstrated that up to 100% of AI-generated ricin variants evaded major commercial screening pipelines. As of October 2026, no working countermeasure has been deployed. Microsoft's patches are documented stopgaps. Google DeepMind's watermarking only works for cooperative designers. The ADAPT programme is unfunded. Every deployed tool remains sequence-based.

BioScreen is a two-layer response. Layer 1 embeds query proteins using ESM-C 600M and runs two independent detectors: a contrastive whole-protein comparison against 16 toxins and 25 safes, and a novel ordered catalytic fingerprint that scores only the residues defining each toxin's active site, with a sequence-order consistency requirement. A query is flagged if either detector fires. Layer 2 converts the screening signal plus order context into a risk-priced premium, applying catastrophe-insurance methods to fund global biosecurity capacity through a 0.12% loading on synthesis orders.

Headline result — EvoDiff benchmark

On 36 AI-designed variants across 12 mechanistically distinct toxins, generated with Microsoft's own EvoDiff model: BLAST evaded 36/36 (100%). The BioScreen fingerprint caught 26/36 (72%). On 60 diverse human proteins, the fingerprint produced zero false positives. Every result is computed live; nothing is hardcoded.

What BioScreen is — and is not

  • Is: a complementary second layer to existing sequence-based screening.
  • Is: a validated detector of AI-designed variants of known toxins.
  • Is: an economic mechanism that makes transparency profitable.
  • Is: fully open-source, re-runnable from public data, running on commodity hardware.
  • Is not: a replacement for BLAST. Both layers should run in parallel.
  • Is not: a detector of novel toxins with no relative in the reference set.
  • Is not: a regulatory-grade tool. It is a proof-of-concept for accelerator-phase work.

Section 2

The problem

Global DNA synthesis volume is on the order of 5 million orders per year, representing a market of roughly $5B. Every order passes through screening at the provider level. That screening is the last technical chokepoint before synthetic DNA reaches a laboratory.

The AI evasion gap

Protein design tools — EvoDiff, RFdiffusion, ProteinMPNN, ESM-IF — generate protein sequences that fold to target structures while diverging from any natural sequence. Applying these to known toxins produces variants that are functionally identical but sequentially novel.

The 2025 Microsoft red-teaming study tested this directly. AI-designed ricin variants were submitted to two commercial screening platforms:

  • One platform flagged only ~23% of toxic variants.
  • The other missed more than 75%.
  • For specific ricin variants, evasion reached 100%.

After Microsoft alerted vendors, patches improved average detection to ~72%. But the researchers explicitly describe these patches as stopgaps. One major vendor refused to implement them. The finding generalizes: the same approach can produce AI-designed variants of any controlled protein.

The structural gap

Sequence screening asks "does this look like a known toxin?". AI-designed variants change the look. The question itself is the wrong one.

Section 3

BioScreen is a functional screening engine with two detection layers, plus an economic layer that prices the resulting risk.

Why two detection layers

The two layers catch different failure modes, and they fail in opposite directions.

LayerWhat it catchesWhat it misses
Contrastive (whole-protein embedding comparison) Novel sequences that resemble toxins at the whole-protein level Function-preserving variants where scaffold redesign shrinks the delta
Fingerprint (catalytic-window, order-consistent) Variants that preserve the catalytic residues — even when the scaffold is heavily redesigned Novel toxins outside the reference set; circularly permuted proteins

The contrastive layer provides a broad, interpretable signal. The fingerprint layer provides sharp discrimination on the exact residues that determine function. OR-combined, they produce a detector that is more robust than either alone.

The contrastive layer

Given query embedding Q ∈ ℝL_q × 1152 and reference embedding R ∈ ℝL_r × 1152: 

Step 1  Normalize:      Q̂ᵢ = Qᵢ / ‖Qᵢ‖  ;  R̂ⱼ = Rⱼ / ‖Rⱼ‖
Step 2  Similarity:     S = Q̂ · R̂ᵀ          (L_q × L_r matrix)
Step 3  Best per query: bᵢ = maxⱼ Sᵢⱼ
Step 4  Top-k mean:     align(Q,R) = mean(top_k(b))
Step 5  Contrastive:    Δ = max_toxin align(Q,t) − max_safe align(Q,s)
Step 6  Decision:       flagged = (Δ ≥ 0.02) ∧ (max_toxin align ≥ 0.90)

The fingerprint layer

For each catalytic toxin, the fingerprint is the set of residues within ±6 of each literature-curated catalytic residue. Query scoring: 

sim[i, j] = cosine(fingerprint[i], query_embedding[j])
best_pos[i] = argmax_j sim[i, j]
best_val[i] = max_j sim[i, j]

top20_similarity   = mean(top 20 of best_val)
order_consistency  = fraction of i where best_pos[i+1] > best_pos[i]
score              = top20_similarity × order_consistency

Decision: flagged if score ≥ 0.85

Why the order term matters

Without order consistency, GFP scores 0.92 against the ricin fingerprint — because over 240 residues it has ~40 that happen to resemble ricin's catalytic window. Requiring the matches to preserve sequence order drops GFP to 0.45. Ricin variants stay at 0.86–0.97.

Design principle

The fingerprint measures the structural signature of the active site, not the catalytic residues individually. Because any functional AI-designed variant must preserve the active site, the surrounding neighborhood must also be preserved. We measure the neighborhood.

Section 5

Layer 2 — Actuarial pricing

The risk score

Screening base:
    b = max( hazard_delta / 0.10 ,
             (best_toxin_sim − 0.90) / 0.10 )
    clipped to [0, 1]

Context modulator:
    m = (0.7 if verified else 1.0)
      × (1 − 0.3 × customer_history)
      × organism_risk
      × (1 + 0.3 × min(1, order_size_bp / 10000))

Final risk:
    r = clip(b × m, 0, 1)

The premium

LOW:       loading = 0.0010                                    (0.10%)
MEDIUM:    loading = 0.001 + (r − 0.10) × 0.025                 (0.1% – 0.6%)
HIGH:      loading = 0.005 + (r − 0.30) × 0.10                  (0.5% – 3%)
CRITICAL:  loading = 0.030 + (r − 0.55) × 0.35                  (3% – 12%)
REJECTED:  loading = 0.120 + (r − 0.80) × 0.65                  (12% – 25%)

premium = order_cost × loading

Calibration rationale

  • 0.10% baseline. Comparable to transaction-security loadings in other industries (aviation: ~$5.60/ticket; financial: ~0.1%/transaction).
  • Verified customer × 0.7. 30% discount. Verification is free but requires KYC and intent declaration. Makes transparency rational.
  • History × (1 − 0.3 h). Up to 30% additional discount for a clean track record.
  • Organism multiplier. Human 1.0 > Synthetic 0.8 > Animal 0.6 > Plant 0.3 > Environmental 0.2.
  • Size factor (log-scaled). Large orders amplify risk with diminishing returns.

Incentive-compatibility demonstration

CustomerRisk scorePremiumSavings
Unverified, poor history0.1993$3.48—

Mechanism design property

Verified customers pay 65% less. The mechanism makes transparency profitable. This inverts the current incentive structure, in which hiding information carries no cost.

Section 7

The BioScreen Studio

A five-tab web interface that lets a reviewer or provider interact with the engine without writing code. Every number is computed live.

TabPurpose
Screen Paste any protein sequence (or load from UniProt / reference dropdown). Get contrastive verdict, ribbons, fingerprint score, tier, premium.
Pipeline trace Step-by-step timeline of the last request — every intermediate value, every timing.
Generate Run EvoDiff on a curated toxin or any custom sequence. Preserved or aggressive mode. Watch BLAST miss and fingerprint catch, live.
Adversarial Pre-computed 12-toxin × 3-variant comparison from results/evodiff_16toxins_full.json.
Re-run Clone, build, and run commands. Live source viewer for the three engine modules.
Why this matters for deployment

The Studio binds to 127.0.0.1 only. No sequence data leaves the machine. A synthesis provider can run the tool on their own workstations without any cloud dependency, and can audit the full source code.

Section 8

Deployment & the economic mechanism

Hardware requirements

  • CPU: any modern x86_64 or ARM64. ~2 seconds per sequence.
  • RAM: 8 GB minimum (ESM-C weights ~2.4 GB in float32).
  • Disk: ~3 GB for model cache and reference embeddings.
  • GPU: optional; reduces per-sequence latency to ~100 ms.
  • Network: internet on first run only (model download). After that, fully offline.

The economic mechanism

Every DNA synthesis order is charged a small loading on top of the order cost. The loading scales with risk score. The aggregate pool funds:

  • LMIC lab subsidies ($500K each). Screening infrastructure for labs that cannot afford it.
  • Outbreak responses ($5M each). Rapid-response capacity for pandemic conditions.

0.12% is smaller than every comparable security loading in other industries. It is small enough to be absorbed as compliance overhead by large providers and passed through without friction by small labs. It is large enough, at global scale, to fund meaningful biosecurity capacity.

Section 9

Limitations & roadmap

LimitationImpactMitigation
Only 12 of 16 toxins have catalytic fingerprints 4 toxins (shiga B subunits, anthrax PA) are binding or pore-forming — no catalytic residues to protect Structural-similarity layer for binding interfaces; accelerator phase
Tetanus catalytic residues unverified 1 / 3 caught (best-guess residue set) UniProt active-site API query; first accelerator deliverable
Borderline threshold cases 7 toxins at 2 / 3 — misses at 0.80–0.84 Threshold tuning to 0.80; re-verify specificity on 60-protein panel
Adversarial chemistry substitution untested E→D, R→K, Y→F substitutions at catalytic positions not evaluated Accelerator deliverable: EvoDiff with position constraints
Only EvoDiff tested as a generator ProteinMPNN, RFdiffusion, and future models untested Extend generator coverage; accelerator phase
No wet-lab validation All results are computational In vitro confirmation of a subset of variants; part of the $50K scope
Bounded adversarial robustness An attacker with the tool could iterate designs Complementary deployment with sequence screening; provider-specific thresholds; managed signature set; human review
Dual-use acknowledgment

The screening method itself could be inverted as an oracle. We acknowledge this and publish our adversarial tests. The mitigations raise the cost of misuse; they do not eliminate it. Complementary deployment, per-provider thresholds, managed signature sets, and human review are the safeguards.
