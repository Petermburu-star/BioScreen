Executive summary

DNA synthesis screening is the primary chokepoint preventing the synthesis of hazardous biological material. Every major provider runs customer orders through sequence-similarity tools. These tools are effective against known sequences. They fail systematically against AI-designed variants that rewrite the sequence while preserving function.

A 2025 Microsoft-led red-teaming study demonstrated that up to 100% of AI-designed ricin variants evaded major commercial screening tools. As of October 2026, no working countermeasure has been deployed. Microsoft's patches are documented stopgaps. Google DeepMind's watermarking only works for cooperative designers. The ADAPT programme is unfunded. Every deployed tool remains sequence-based.

BioScreen implements an ordered catalytic fingerprint: a compact representation of the residues that determine a toxin's chemistry, scored with a sequence-order consistency constraint. On five EvoDiff-generated ricin variants that BLAST cannot detect (local alignment 0.18–0.20), the fingerprint catches all five (scores 0.86–0.97). On 60 human proteins, it produces zero false positives.

Headline result

Five ricin A-chain variants generated by Microsoft's own EvoDiff model, with 0.7–1.5% sequence identity to native, evaded BLAST completely (scores 0.176–0.203). Our ordered catalytic fingerprint caught all five (scores 0.86–0.97). A circularity control — variants where the catalytic window is destroyed — correctly clears at the same rate as human proteins. This is the first published demonstration of a functional screening method catching EvoDiff-generated variants that sequence screening misses.

The problem

Global DNA synthesis volume is on the order of 5 million orders per year, representing a market of roughly $5B. Every order passes through screening at the provider level. That screening infrastructure is the last technical chokepoint before synthetic DNA reaches a laboratory.

How sequence screening works

A customer submits a DNA sequence. The provider translates it to a protein (or keeps it as DNA) and runs a similarity search against a reference set of controlled pathogens, toxins, and regulatory watchlist entries. Hits above a similarity threshold trigger a flag.

Where it breaks

A 2025 Microsoft-led red-team study applied AI protein design to 72 controlled proteins including ricin and botulinum. Variants retained their fold and function while diverging in sequence. When tested against two commercial screening platforms:

  • One platform flagged only ~23% of toxic variants.
  • The other missed more than 75%.
  • For specific ricin variants, evasion reached 100%.

After Microsoft alerted vendors, patches improved average detection to ~72%. But the researchers explicitly describe these patches as stopgaps. One major vendor refused to implement them. And the finding generalizes: the same approach can produce AI-designed variants of any controlled protein.

Method

BioScreen is a functional screening engine with two components: an embedding pipeline and an ordered fingerprint scoring function.

Embedding

Query proteins are embedded with ESM-C 600M (EvolutionaryScale, MIT license). The full per-residue matrix (L, 1152) is preserved. Each residue keeps its own 1152-dimensional vector.

Fingerprint extraction

For each toxin in the reference set, we identify the residues that differ most from a safe-protein background. The safe background is the pooled per-residue embeddings of 25 diverse human and lab proteins. The 42 residues with the lowest maximum similarity to the safe background become the toxin's fingerprint.

Ordered scoring

For a query, we compute: 

sim[i, j] = cosine( fingerprint[i], query_embedding[j] )
best_pos[i] = argmax_j sim[i, j]
best_val[i] = max_j sim[i, j]

top20_similarity = mean( top 20 values of best_val )
order_consistency = fraction of i where best_pos[i+1] > best_pos[i]

score = top20_similarity × order_consistency

A query is flagged if its score against any toxin fingerprint exceeds 0.85.

Why the order term matters

Without order consistency, safe proteins score high. GFP scores 0.92 on per-residue similarity to the ricin fingerprint — because over 240 residues, GFP has some 42 residues that happen to resemble ricin's catalytic window. Requiring those matches to appear in the correct sequence order eliminates this. GFP's ordered score drops to 0.45 — below any toxin variant, well clear of the threshold.

2. Circularity control

The EvoDiff variants preserved the catalytic window we used to build the fingerprint. To rule out circularity, we generated aggressive variants where EvoDiff redesigns the entire A-chain — including the catalytic window. If our fingerprint were only checking that we preserved what we preserved, these would also score high.

Limitations

We list limitations without qualification. Every panel should ask about these.

LimitationImpactMitigation / next step
Only ricin tested end-to-end with EvoDiff variants The other three toxins have validated fingerprints but haven't been tested against their own EvoDiff variants Accelerator deliverable: extend EvoDiff validation to all 16 toxins
Adversarial robustness untested An attacker who knows about the fingerprint could attempt chemistry-preserving substitutions (E→D, R→K, Y→F) Accelerator deliverable: generate these variants, measure whether the fingerprint catches them
Function of EvoDiff variants not experimentally validated The design preserves the catalytic window, but we haven't confirmed the variants fold and catalyze correctly Wet-lab validation as part of the $50K scope
Only one design tool tested EvoDiff is the specific tool Microsoft used. Other tools may produce variants with different characteristics Extend testing to ProteinMPNN, RFdiffusion, and other sequence-to-function models
Narrow scope relative to the problem BioScreen catches function-preserving variants of toxins already in a reference set. It does not detect novel toxins or novel mechanisms Honest scoping: this is a solution to the specific threat Microsoft identified, not a general solution to AI-designed biothreats
Dual-use acknowledgment

The screening method itself could be inverted as an oracle: an attacker with the tool could iterate designs to minimize the fingerprint score. We publish our own adversarial tests. Per-provider thresholds, a managed reference set, and human review for high-risk orders are the mitigations.

Roadmap

Accelerator phase — 6 months

  • Months 1–2 — EvoDiff validation across all 16 toxins. Currently 1/16 end-to-end. Target: 16/16 validation JSONs.
  • Months 2–3 — Adversarial robustness test. Chemistry-preserving substitutions. Report the boundary honestly.
  • Months 3–4 — Industry pilot. One synthesis provider or CRO. Real orders, real screening, real results.
  • Months 5–6 — Publication and regulatory engagement. bioRxiv preprint. Initial conversations with WHO or BWC ISU.

Beyond the accelerator — 12–24 months

  • Production deployment alongside existing sequence screening.
  • Regulatory recognition as a valid screening signal.
  • First LMIC lab subsidies disbursed through the pricing mechanism.
  • Community-governed reference set with a scientific advisory board.
  • Extension to benchtop synthesis platforms.
