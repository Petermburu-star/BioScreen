The problem

Sequence screening is structurally blind to AI-designed variants

  • DNA synthesis screening relies on sequence similarity to known hazards.
  • AI protein design can rewrite a toxin's sequence while preserving its function.
  • A 2025 Microsoft-led red-teaming study demonstrated that up to 100% of AI-designed ricin variants evaded major commercial screening tools.
  • The same weakness enables order fragmentation and benchtop synthesis.
The structural gap

Sequence screening asks "does this look like a known toxin?" — AI-designed variants change the look while keeping the function.

Layer 1 — Two independent detection methods

ESM fingerprint + context-free fallback

Every existing tool compares whole proteins. That fails because at the whole-protein level, every folded protein resembles every other.

We run two detectors and OR them together:

1. Contrastive — whole-protein comparison Δ = max_toxin_similarity − max_safe_similarity fires if Δ ≥ 0.02 AND toxin ≥ 0.90 2. Fingerprint — catalytic-window, order-consistent top-20 residue similarity × sequence-order consistency fires if score ≥ 0.85

Why the fingerprint works

Every functional variant must preserve the active site

An AI-designed ricin variant can rewrite 570 of 576 residues. It cannot remove the four catalytic residues — Y80, Y123, E177, R180 — without destroying the chemistry that makes ricin toxic.

We extract the residues within ±6 of each catalytic site and score queries on both per-residue similarity and sequence-order consistency.

The discriminator

GFP scores 0.92 on per-residue similarity alone — because over 240 residues, some 40 resemble ricin's window. Requiring order drops GFP to 0.45. Ricin variants stay at 0.86–0.97.

Honest limitations

What we know we don't know

  • Only 13 of 17 toxins have fingerprints. Includes Pseudomonas ExoA (P11439) added recently. The other 4 are binding or pore-forming — no catalytic residues to protect. A structural layer handles them in the accelerator phase.
  • ESM fingerprint catches 51.3%. Embedding-based fingerprints are context-sensitive — the embedding shifts when the scaffold is redesigned, even if the catalytic residues are preserved.
  • Context-free layer catches 39/39 preserved, 0/39 aggressive. Invariant to scaffold redesign. Adversarial variants (preserve residues, break geometry) remain untested.
  • Adversarial chemistry substitution untested (E→D, R→K, Y→F).
  • Only EvoDiff tested as a generator.
  • No wet-lab validation. All results computational. In vitro confirmation is part of the $50K scope.
