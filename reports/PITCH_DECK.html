The problem

DNA synthesis is screened by sequence similarity. AI protein generators now produce variants of known toxins at 1–25% identity — scaffold fully redesigned, functional motif intact. Similarity screeners lose the signal.

And facilities that can't phone home — air-gapped labs — have no cloud screener at all.

The solution

Detect the conserved catalytic chemistry, not the sequence. A gated functional fingerprint: catalytic identity is a precondition, not a weight — engineered so safe proteins mathematically cannot fire.

Every catalytic residue in the enzymatic database is verified against UniProt feature annotations, and every native score matches its first-principles prediction to 4 decimals.

We prove it live — and the tool polices itself

The reviewer picks the toxin and the seed. Variants are generated in the session, hashed, confirmed absent from every dataset, and scored with full decomposition.

Controls enforce themselves: preserved must flag, destroyed must clear — or the tool prints DEMO INVALIDATED on itself. We ship the run where it did exactly that.

Honest boundary — and the funded answer

Substituting the entire catalytic core conservatively evades detection — and those substitutions typically destroy the toxin's chemistry (Y→F removes the reactive hydroxyl; HExxH→KExxR kills zinc coordination). We map our evasion space instead of hiding it.

Acceleration funds: the structural layer — ESMFold with pLDDT-gated catalytic geometry, already prototyped — closing that boundary entirely.

Business model

Screening signal → actuarial risk pricing: a 0.12% loading on synthesis orders funds a global biosecurity pool (pricing module validated).

The moat is the deployment model: runs during a synthesis order review, offline, on existing hardware. No model downloads, no cloud dependency, no GPU.
